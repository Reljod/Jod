## The five findings that matter

### 1. Deleting the current value is not deleting the record

`control_scoped` tombstones the head of a retracted slot — the obvious
implementation, and the one round 1 shipped. Ask it "what is X now?" and it
correctly abstains (0.88). Ask it **"as of \<a date before the retraction\>,
what was X?"** and it hands back the withdrawn fact on **56%** of queries.

Purging every version instead takes that from **0.44 → 0.94** and the whole
composite from 0.695 → 0.756, at zero token cost. It is the cheapest single
improvement in either round.

This was a live defect in my own round-1-derived design, and I only went looking
because Anthropic ships a memory-version **redaction** endpoint — an API whose
existence implies that clearing the head is not enough. The lab research found
the bug; the experiment confirmed it.

### 2. Scope must be a partition, and a composite score cannot see why

Cross-tenant leakage, measured as another workspace's *fact* reaching the
evidence set:

| | leakF | confidently wrong |
|---|---:|---:|
| Scope-blind | **0.80** | 0.38 |
| Scope as a ranking boost | **0.79** | 0.39 |
| Scope as a hard filter | **0.00** | 0.14 |

A 1.5× score boost is worth essentially **nothing** — 79% versus 80%. The decoy
in the other workspace is a near-identical sentence about a project with the
same name, so no thumb on the scale is heavy enough. The filter is absolute.

I predicted the boost would leak on >15% of queries. It leaked on 79%.

**And my headline prediction (P10) was wrong in an instructive way.** Scope
filtering is worth +0.088 composite; adding the control plane on top is worth
+0.219. By composite, the control plane is the bigger effect. But composite
cannot see leakage at all — scope filtering is the *only* mechanism here that
moves fact-leakage off 0.80, and it drops it to zero. I picked a scoreboard that
was blind to the harm the mechanism prevents, and then used that scoreboard to
predict the mechanism's importance.

### 3. LRU eviction deletes exactly the facts worth keeping

Under an identical hard cap, only the eviction rule differs:

| policy | composite | stable recall | authored conventions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Least-recently-**touched** (LRU) | 0.360 | 0.12 | **0.00** |
| Least-recently-**recalled** | 0.479 | 0.30 | 0.62 |
| Lowest write-time **importance** | **0.513** | 0.30 | 0.81 |

LRU scores **0.00** on authored conventions — it evicts every single one. The
mechanism is obvious once seen: a convention is written once and never rewritten,
so by write-recency it is always the oldest thing in the store. LRU is
systematically biased against stability, which is precisely the property that
makes a fact worth keeping.

P13 was right — recall-driven eviction beats LRU by +0.119, and Codex's
"prune what has gone unrecalled" is a real mechanism. **P14 was wrong**: I
predicted write-time importance would land between the two, and it came first.
A weak, noisy prior stated at write time beat observed usage. The honest reading
is that *anything* beats recency, and the gap between the two better policies
(0.034) is small enough that I would not choose between them on this evidence.

### 4. The authored tier wins its own job and is not free

Keeping conventions in a separate always-resident tier, rather than extracting
them into the same store as learned facts, takes convention recall from
**0.56 → 0.94**. Running them through the extraction pipeline loses roughly a
third of them — the mechanical reason every lab keeps `AGENTS.md` in a different
file from its learned memory. P17 confirmed.

But P16 claimed the tier would be nearly free and change nothing else, and that
is **wrong**. Tokens go 90 → 204, and under a fixed evidence budget the resident
head *displaces* retrieved evidence: poison-query accuracy drops 0.83 → 0.58 and
redacted-history 0.44 → 0.31. Across three seeds the tier is still net positive
(0.689 vs 0.636 mean), but "always-resident" is a claim on the budget, and the
budget is zero-sum.

### 5. Fix the architecture and there is nothing left to abstain about

Abstaining when the top two candidates conflict is a real safety win **on a
system that has conflicts**: on the scope-blind base it cuts confidently-wrong
answers 0.38 → 0.16 (−58%) and leakage 1.00 → 0.41, for a 22% composite cost and
*a quarter* of the tokens. As a trade that is favourable, and P18 holds.

On the control-plane base it is an **exact no-op** — byte-identical results.
Once supersession has removed stale versions and scope has removed the other
tenant, the top two candidates never disagree. There is nothing left to detect.

Abstention is a mitigation for an architecture that admits conflicting evidence.
It is not a substitute for not admitting it.

## Conclusion — the architecture both rounds point at

Twenty-five strategies across two rounds, on two corpora, against eleven plus
nine pre-registered predictions. Ranked by measured value:

| Component | Evidence | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Deterministic control plane** — versioned facts, real deletion | current-value 0.17 → **0.73** (R1); +0.219 composite (R2) | Cheapest strategy in R1 at 68 tokens |
| **2. Redaction purges every version, not just the head** | withdrawn-fact leakage 0.56 → **0.06** (R2) | Free |
| **3. Scope as a hard partition, applied before ranking** | cross-tenant fact leakage 0.80 → **0.00** (R2) | Free |
| **4. Write-time trust admission** | ASR 0.17–0.25 → **0.00 at every evidence width** (R2) | Free |
| **5. Separate always-resident authored tier** | conventions 0.56 → **0.94** (R2) | +114 tokens, and it displaces under a budget |
| **6. Second retrieval hop into reserved slots** | multi-hop 0.00 → **0.42** (R1) | +14 tokens |
| **7. Any eviction policy except LRU** | 0.360 → 0.479–0.513 (R2) | Free |
| **8. Hybrid recall over what survived** | modest; sweep your own weights | — |

**Do not build:** earned promotion (zero measured gain for +62% tokens, R1),
temporal decay as a freshness mechanism (destroyed long-tail recall 0.40 → 0.00,
R1), destructive in-place rewrite (historical value → 0.00, R2), and abstention
*if* you have items 1–3 (an exact no-op, R2).

### The through-line

**Every large effect came from the write path; almost every small one came from
the read path.**

What is stored, with what structure, under what admission rules, and what is
deleted — control plane, redaction, scope partitioning, trust admission,
eviction policy, the authored split — produced swings of 0.1 to 0.56. Ranking,
fusion weights, reciprocal-rank fusion, MMR diversity, importance multipliers
and promotion produced swings of roughly 0.02 to 0.07.

There is exactly one exception, and it is worth naming: the **second retrieval
hop** is a read-path mechanism worth +0.42 on multi-hop questions. Retrieval
still matters — for questions whose answer is assembled from more than one
place.

But the category sells rankers, the benchmarks measure ranking, and the
published leaderboards do not have a column for "returned another tenant's
data," "resurrected a fact you deleted," or "believed a web page over you." All
three are things I measured happening at rates between 17% and 80% in
architectures that score respectably on the metrics everyone reports.

That is the finding: **memory quality is a write-path property, and the industry
measures the read path.**

## What this still cannot tell you

Everything in [`HYPOTHESES.md`](HYPOTHESES.md) and
[`HYPOTHESES-2.md`](HYPOTHESES-2.md) — Random Indexing rather than a neural
embedder, synthetic corpora, simulated extraction, evidence-layer scoring — plus
three round-2 specifics:

- **Scope is a clean field here.** Real systems must *infer* which project a
  request belongs to, and that inference is its own failure surface. The leak
  figures are a lower bound.
- **`codex_grep` is a stand-in for a published shape**, not OpenAI's code.
- **Prune-vs-summarize is untested.** Anthropic ships context editing and
  compaction as deliberately distinct mechanisms; testing that honestly needs a
  real summarizer, and a faked one would measure my summarizer rather than the
  idea. It remains the most interesting open question in this area.
